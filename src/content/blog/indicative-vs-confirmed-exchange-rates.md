If you have ever compared the dollar to naira rate across three websites and got three different numbers, you have met the difference between an **indicative** rate and a **confirmed** rate.

## What an indicative rate is

An indicative rate is a reference. It tells you roughly where the market or a desk is at a moment in time. It is useful for planning and budgeting, but nobody is committing to deal at that level. The [rates on the Zender site](/fx-rates) are indicative desk references.

## What a confirmed rate is

A confirmed rate is the level a desk agrees to deal at, for a specific amount, currency, and time window. It is what your payment is actually priced at. You get one by talking to the desk about your real transaction.

## Why the two differ

- **Amount.** Larger and smaller amounts can be priced differently.
- **Timing.** Rates move through the day; a reference from this morning may not hold this afternoon.
- **Currency and corridor.** Dollar, pound, euro, yuan, and dirham conversions are not priced identically.
- **Source.** Banks, desks, and data sites all publish at different times and with different spreads.

## How to use each one

Use indicative rates to plan: estimate what an invoice will cost in naira, compare options, and decide when to start a conversation. Use a confirmed rate to act: never commit funds based on an indicative number alone.
