Paying a supplier abroad from Nigeria comes down to three things: knowing exactly what the invoice asks for, converting naira at a level you have agreed in advance, and getting the funds to the supplier before their deadline. This guide walks through each step.

## 1. Read the invoice before you think about rates

Start with the supplier's invoice or proforma invoice and check:

- **The currency they bill in.** Chinese factories often bill in US dollars or yuan, Dubai sellers in dirham or dollars, UK suppliers in pounds, and eurozone suppliers in euros.
- **The exact amount**, including any deposit or balance split.
- **The supplier's legal name and country**, as it should appear on the payment.
- **The deadline.** When does the supplier need to *see* the funds to release production or shipment?

Getting these right first avoids the most common problem: paying the wrong amount, in the wrong currency, too late.

## 2. Choose the currency with your supplier

If your supplier accepts more than one currency, ask which they prefer and whether their bank charges differ. Paying in the supplier's home currency can avoid a second conversion on their side, but not every supplier is set up for it.

## 3. Get an indicative rate, then a confirmed one

Rates you see online, including the [indicative rates on this site](/fx-rates), are references. The rate for your actual payment depends on the amount, the currency, and the timing. Ask your desk for a confirmed rate for your specific amount before any funds move, and agree how long that rate holds.

## 4. Plan backwards from the supplier's deadline

Work backwards from the date the supplier needs the funds. Leave room for compliance checks, bank cut-off times, and public holidays in both countries. If your shipment is time-sensitive, say so at the start of the conversation.

## 5. Keep your paper trail

Keep the invoice, your payment confirmation, and any correspondence together. If you import through a Nigerian bank, you may also need documents such as a Form M for eligible imports; check the requirements for your goods with your bank.
